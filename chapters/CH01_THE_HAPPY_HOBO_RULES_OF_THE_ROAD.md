## Preamble: The Reality of the Grid

Forget the heavy grain carts, the bloated postal bureaucracies, and the illusion of uniform speed. The universe isn't an infinite calculus sheet; it's a bounded, discrete topological graph. Language isn't a wall of mutual unintelligibility—it's a continuous network where distance is measured by path length and phonetic drift.

To map the grid, you don't need a standing army. You need hobos, a walking stick, a hobo rig, a book, a punch card, and a bit of Blanche DuBois.

---

## The Five Rules of the Open Road

1. **Rule 1 — Pack Light, Live Off the Land:** Centralized logistics will bankrupt any empire. Bring a walking stick, an `18.5 W` hobo rig, and a light heart (`V* = 2/3`). Let the local economy fund your journey.
2. **Rule 2 — The Open Academy (The Recursive Loop):** The central yard is not a military barracks; it's a hobo training camp. Hand new recruits the rules of the road, a book, and a punch card. When they return from the frontier seasoned and wise, they teach the next wave.
3. **Rule 3 — The Gossip Protocol (P2P Synchronization):** When you cross paths with another traveler by a campfire or a crossroads, stop and trade notes. Compare punch cards, punch new holes to sync discoveries, and pass along the lay of the land.
4. **Rule 4 — Read the Signs and Honor the Horizons:** Do not waste steps on dead ends or re-tread paths already walked. When you hit Village Z—the horizon where the local dialect drops off a cliff into pure phonetic gibberish—mark it as a structural tripwire on your punch card and route around the edge.
5. **Rule 5 — Punch Cards Are Your Hard Drive:** Compress your journey into Thomas Fowler 5-trit/byte holes on an 8-byte card (`3^5 = 243 <= 256`). Slip your card into the junction slot for the next wanderer to read, or carry it back home in your pack.
